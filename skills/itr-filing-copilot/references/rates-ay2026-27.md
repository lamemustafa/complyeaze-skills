# AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26) — rates, thresholds and deadlines

**Shape, not truth.** Re-verify every figure against a primary source before
relying on it. Rates change annually and mid-year amendments happen. Each row
below carries its section so you can check it in one lookup at
`incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-<n>`.

Last verified against primary sources: **28 July 2026**.

---

## Which Act governs this return

**AY 2026-27 = FY 2025-26 = the Income-tax Act, 1961.**

The **Income-tax Act, 2025** commences **1 April 2026** and repeals the 1961 Act
from that date, with savings for prior years. Its first "tax year" is **2026-27**
(the financial year beginning 1 April 2026), so the first returns under the new
Act are filed in **2027**.

Practical consequences while filing in mid-2026:

- The return you are filing now is a **1961 Act** return. Cite 1961 Act sections.
- **Advance tax from June 2026 onward** — for tax year 2026-27 — is computed
  under the **2025 Act**. The same taxpayer is simultaneously under two Acts.
- The e-filing portal runs **both modules concurrently**. If a portal screen
  quotes a section number you do not recognise, check whether you have drifted
  into the 2025 Act module.
- s.536 of the 2025 Act preserves proceedings, assessments, appeals and
  liabilities arising under the 1961 Act.

Source: incometax.gov.in → Help → "Objective and scope of the new Act" FAQ.

| Slab | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to basic exemption | Nil |
| Basic exemption – ₹5,00,000 | 5% (an empty band for the 80+ group, whose exemption is already ₹5,00,000 — there is no 5% slab for super seniors) |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

| Age at any time in FY 2025-26 | Limit |
|---|---|
| Below 60 | ₹2,50,000 |
| 60 to 79 (senior citizen) | ₹3,00,000 |
| 80 and above (super senior) | ₹5,00,000 |

- Rebate u/s 87A: **₹12,500**, total income up to **₹5,00,000**, **no marginal relief**

## Capital gains

| Head | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| STCG u/s 111A (equity / equity MF, STT paid) | **20%** | flat, whole year |
| LTCG u/s 112A (same, held >12 months) | **12.5%** | first **₹1,25,000** exempt |
| LTCG u/s 112 — assets other than land/building | **12.5%** | no indexation, no option |
| LTCG u/s 112 — **land or building** | **12.5% or 20%** | see the dual computation below |
| Specified mutual funds / debt (s.50AA) | slab | deemed short-term regardless of holding |
| VDA u/s 115BBH | **30%** | no expenses, no set-off, losses not carried forward |
| Unexplained credits u/s 115BBE | **60%** | + 25% surcharge; no deduction or set-off |

### Land and building — the dual computation (second proviso to s.112(1)(a))

Where a long-term capital asset being **land or building or both** was
**acquired before 23 July 2024** and **transferred on or after** that date:

> tax = **lower of** (12.5% on the gain computed *without* indexation)
> and (20% on the gain computed *with* indexation)

Read the mechanics carefully — most write-ups get this wrong:

- **It is a comparison of tax, not a re-computation of gain.** The *unindexed*
  gain is what enters total income either way. Only the tax figure is capped.
- Consequently, if the indexed computation produces a **loss**, that loss is
  **not available** — the benefit is capped at reducing tax, never at creating a
  set-off.
- The proviso sits inside **clause (a)**, which opens *"in the case of an
  individual or a Hindu undivided family, being a resident"*. So it is
  **residents only** — not NRIs, firms, LLPs, AOP/BOI or companies.
- It covers **land and building only**. Unlisted shares, gold, debt funds and
  every other asset go to 12.5% with no fallback.
- The comparison is made **after** any s.54 / 54F exemption.
- ITR-2 Validation Rule 572 for AY 2026-27 encodes exactly this and confirms
  non-residents get 12.5% with no option.

`scripts/compute_tax.py` **refuses** to compute this case unless you give it both
the indexed and the unindexed gain. That refusal is deliberate: silently applying
12.5% can overtax a property sale by a wide margin.

### Basic exemption absorption — residents only

Where total income *reduced by* these gains falls **below the basic exemption
limit**, the shortfall is set against the gains before the special rate applies:

| Income | Provision |
|---|---|
| s.111A STCG | **first proviso to s.111A(1)** |
| s.112A LTCG | **proviso to s.112A(2)** |
| s.112 LTCG | **first proviso to s.112(1)(a)** |

Do **not** cite s.111A(2) / s.112A(5) / s.112(2) for this — those deal with
Chapter VI-A deductions, which is a different restriction (see below).

- Applies under **both** regimes; under the new regime the limit is ₹4,00,000.
- Order for 112A: absorb the basic exemption **first**, then the ₹1,25,000
  exemption, then charge 12.5%.
- **No equivalent exists** for 115BB / 115BBJ / 115BBH / 115BBE.

### Chapter VI-A cannot be set against special-rate income

s.111A(2), s.112A(5) and s.112(2): Chapter VI-A deductions are allowed from
gross total income **as reduced by** the special-rate gains. A taxpayer whose
income is entirely capital gains gets no benefit from 80C at all.

### Buyback from 1 October 2024

- **s.2(22)(f)**: the whole buyback consideration is a **deemed dividend**,
  taxable in the shareholder's hands as **Income from Other Sources** at slab
  rates. No deduction for cost (s.57 allows only the 20% interest deduction).
- **s.46A proviso**: the value of consideration for capital-gains purposes is
  **deemed nil**.
- Reporting: Schedule CG with **full value of consideration = 0** and **cost =
  actual cost** → the entire cost emerges as a **capital loss** (short- or
  long-term by holding period). The dividend goes in **Schedule OS** with TDS
  u/s 194 at 10% claimed in Schedule TDS.
- The loss **cannot** be set off against the buyback dividend itself. Carry
  forward under s.74 for **8 assessment years** — and only if the return is
  filed **within the s.139(1) due date** (s.80).
- s.115QA (company-level 20% buyback tax) and s.10(34A) both ceased for buybacks
  on or after 1 October 2024.

## s.87A rebate against special-rate income — the live divergence

This is the single most contested computation on the portal. Get the regime
right before you assert anything.

**New regime — settled.** The **second proviso to s.87A**, inserted by the
Finance Act 2025 with effect from AY 2026-27, reads:

> "Provided further that the deduction under the first proviso shall not exceed
> the amount of income-tax payable as per the rates provided in sub-section (1A)
> of section 115BAC."

Tax on 111A / 112A / 112 does not arise "as per the rates provided in
115BAC(1A)". So the rebate **reaches slab-rate tax only** and never touches
special-rate income. Note that total income for the ₹12,00,000 *eligibility*
test still **includes** those gains — it is only the *quantum* of rebate that is
confined.

**Old regime — allowed against 111A, barred against 112A.**

- **s.112A(6)** is an express bar: *"…the rebate under section 87A shall be
  allowed from the income-tax on the total income as reduced by tax payable on
  such capital gains."*
- **s.111A contains no equivalent.** s.111A(2) restricts only Chapter VI-A;
  s.111A(3) is omitted. Where Parliament intended exclusion it said so.
- ITAT Ahmedabad, *Jayshreeben Jayantibhai Palsana v. ITO* (Aug 2025) so held.

**`[UNVERIFIED]` — what the AY 2026-27 utility actually does under the old
regime.** The published ITR-2 validation rules are silent (Rules 485, 533, 534,
535 cover only the ₹12,500 cap, non-residents, HUFs and the ₹5L ceiling); the
tax-computation engine is not published. History is not encouraging — from
**5 July 2024** the utility silently stopped allowing 87A against special-rate
income, the Bombay High Court PIL (*Chamber of Tax Consultants v. DGIT
(Systems)*, final order 24 Jan 2025) forced only a *procedural* fix, and CBDT
never conceded the substantive point.

**Operational rule: compute it, then read the portal's own figure. If they
differ, tell the taxpayer both numbers and what the difference turns on. Do not
assert that the portal is wrong.**

## Surcharge

| Total income | Old regime | New regime |
|---|---|---|
| > ₹50 lakh ≤ ₹1 crore | 10% | 10% |
| > ₹1 crore ≤ ₹2 crore | 15% | 15% |
| > ₹2 crore ≤ ₹5 crore | 25% | **25%** |
| > ₹5 crore | **37%** | **25% — the 37% band does not exist** |

⚠️ Several widely circulated AY 2026-27 rate charts wrongly show **37%** for the
new regime. The department's own tax-rates page states the 25% cap plainly.

**15% cap on part of the income.** First Schedule, Paragraph A proviso:

> "…in case where the total income includes any income by way of dividend or
> income chargeable under sections 111A, 112 and 112A…, the rate of surcharge on
> the income-tax calculated on that part of income shall not exceed fifteen per
> cent."

So surcharge on the dividend + 111A + 112 + 112A slice is capped at 15%; the
residual income bears the full 25%/37%. Departmental guidance extends the same
treatment to s.115AD(1)(b).

**Carve-out, `[documented, not statutory]`:** incometax.gov.in guidance says the
15% cap does **not** apply where income is taxable under **s.115A, 115AB, 115AC,
115ACA or 115E** — those bear the enhanced rate. The Finance Act First Schedule
provisos do not name these sections, so treat this as departmental practice
rather than express statutory text.

**Marginal relief** applies at each surcharge threshold (₹50L, ₹1cr, ₹2cr, ₹5cr):
tax + surcharge on the actual income may not exceed tax + surcharge at the
threshold plus the income above it. Cess is **not** covered by marginal relief.

## Who must file even below the exemption limit

**Seventh proviso to s.139(1)** — mandatory filing if the person:

| Trigger | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Deposits in one or more **current accounts** | **exceeds ₹1 crore** |
| **Foreign travel** spend, self or any other person | **exceeds ₹2 lakh** |
| **Electricity** consumption | **exceeds ₹1 lakh** |

**Rule 12AB** (Notification 37/2022), for persons other than a company or firm:

| Trigger | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Business turnover / gross receipts | **exceeds ₹60 lakh** |
| Professional gross receipts | **exceeds ₹10 lakh** |
| Aggregate **TDS + TCS**, age < 60 | **₹25,000 or more** |
| Aggregate **TDS + TCS**, age ≥ 60 | **₹50,000 or more** |
| Aggregate deposits in **savings** accounts | **₹50 lakh or more** |

Note the wording split: the first set are *exceeds*, the TDS/TCS and savings rows
are *or more*.

**Fourth proviso to s.139(1)** — a **resident** holding any foreign asset, having
signing authority in a foreign account, or being a beneficiary of a foreign
asset **must file regardless of income**, and must complete Schedule FA.

## House property

- **Two** properties may be treated as self-occupied with **nil annual value** —
  s.23(2) read with s.23(4). The two-house limit came in with Finance Act 2019
  and is unchanged.
- **Finance Act 2025 relaxed the condition** (effective AY 2025-26, so live for
  AY 2026-27): nil annual value now applies where the owner occupies the house
  *or cannot actually occupy it "due to any reason"* — replacing the old
  requirement that the non-occupation be caused by employment or business
  elsewhere. A second home kept vacant for family or health reasons now
  qualifies without contrivance.
- s.23(3) still denies nil value if the house is let for any part of the year.
- **s.24(b) interest, old regime:** ₹2,00,000 cap for self-occupied — a
  **combined** cap across both properties, not per property. ₹30,000 where the
  loan predates 1 April 1999, is not for acquisition/construction, or
  construction was not completed within 5 years.
- **Let-out property:** interest fully deductible, but house-property loss set
  off against other heads is capped at **₹2,00,000** a year (s.71(3A)); the
  balance carries forward 8 years.
- **New regime:** no interest deduction for a self-occupied property
  (s.115BAC(2)(i)); let-out interest is still deductible in computing that
  property's income, but the resulting loss can be set off against **nothing**
  and cannot be carried forward (s.115BAC(2)(ii)).

## Presumptive taxation

| | s.44AD (business) | s.44ADA (profession) |
|---|---|---|
| Base limit | ₹2 crore | ₹50 lakh |
| Enhanced limit | **₹3 crore** | **₹75 lakh** |
| Presumptive income | **8%**, or **6%** on receipts by account-payee cheque/draft/ECS/prescribed electronic mode received on or before the s.139(1) due date | **50%** |

**The 5% test is on RECEIPTS ONLY.** Both sections read *"where the amount or
aggregate of the amounts **received** during the previous year, in cash, does not
exceed five per cent of the total turnover or gross receipts"*. Payments are
irrelevant. A cheque that is **not** account-payee counts as cash (Explanation).

Do not import the s.44AB(a) test here — *that* one (₹1 crore → ₹10 crore) requires
cash **receipts ≤ 5% AND** cash **payments ≤ 5%**. Two different tests, commonly
conflated.

The 6%/8% split is **per rupee of receipt**, not all-or-nothing: a mixed business
applies 6% to the digital slice and 8% to the rest.

s.44AD(4)/(5): opting out after opting in locks the taxpayer out for **5
subsequent years** and triggers a s.44AB audit if income exceeds the exemption
limit.

## Advance tax, interest and fees

- Advance tax is due only if the liability is **₹10,000 or more** (s.208).
  Below that, **234B and 234C are nil regardless of quarterly allocation.**
- **s.234A** — 1% per month or part on unpaid tax, from the day after the due
  date to the date of furnishing.
- **s.234B** — 1% per month or part where advance tax paid < 90% of assessed tax,
  from 1 April of the AY.
- **s.234C** — 1% per month for 3 months on shortfalls at 15 Jun (15%), 15 Sep
  (45%), 15 Dec (75%), and 1% for one month at 15 Mar (100%). Safe harbours:
  no interest if ≥12% paid by 15 Jun and ≥36% by 15 Sep. Capital gains, lottery,
  115BBH and first-year business income are excluded from the earlier
  instalments if paid in the remaining ones.
- **s.234F** — late-filing fee: **₹1,000** if total income ≤ ₹5,00,000, else
  **₹5,000**. (The `/w/section-234f` bare-act page still serves the superseded
  2017 text with ₹10,000 — use `/w/section-234f-11`.)
- **s.234-I** — new, see below.
- **s.244A** — interest on refunds; it is taxable income under Other Sources.

`scripts/compute_tax.py` computes 234F and 234-I from a `--filing-date`. It
deliberately does **not** compute 234A/B/C — read those off the portal.

## Deadlines for AY 2026-27

The Finance Act 2026 substituted **Explanation 2 to s.139(1)** and **s.139(5)**,
both deemed effective **1 March 2026** — so they govern this filing season.

| Category | Due date | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| No business or professional income | **31 July 2026** | Expl. 2(d) |
| Business/profession, accounts **not** liable to audit u/s 44AB | **31 August 2026** ⚠️ new | Expl. 2(c) |
| Accounts liable to audit u/s 44AB; companies; working partner of an audited firm | **31 October 2026** | Expl. 2(b) |
| s.92E transfer-pricing report required | **30 November 2026** | Expl. 2(a) |
| Belated return u/s 139(4) | **31 December 2026** | unchanged |
| Revised return u/s 139(5) | **31 March 2027** ⚠️ changed | s.139(5) as substituted |
| Updated return u/s 139(8A) | **31 March 2031** | 48 months from end of AY |

**The 31 August slot is keyed on audit liability, not on the ITR form.** An
ITR-3 filer who is audit-liable gets 31 October; an ITR-2 filer always gets
31 July, whatever their income size. Do not reason from the form number.

Belated and revised deadlines are now **asymmetric**: revision remains possible
for three months after the belated window closes.

**s.234-I** (inserted into the 1961 Act by the Finance Act 2026, clause 12,
deemed effective 1 March 2026):

> "…where any person furnishes a return of income under sub-section (5) of
> section 139, beyond nine months but before twelve months from the end of the
> relevant assessment year, he shall pay by way of a fee — (a) ₹1,000 if total
> income does not exceed ₹5,00,000; (b) ₹5,000 in any other case."

⚠️ **Drafting defect.** Read literally the section is unreachable: s.139(5) closes
the revision window at the **end of the assessment year**, while s.234-I charges
a fee for filings 9–12 months **after** that. "Assessment year" is plainly meant
to be "previous year" — the twin provision in the Income-tax Act 2025 (s.428(b))
runs the clock from the end of the **tax year**. The utility implements the
intended reading: ITR-1 validation rules 324/328 and ITR-2 rules 694/695 charge
the fee where *"ITR is filed after 31/12/2026 and filing section is 139(5)"*.

**Operative rule:** a revised return filed **1 Jan – 31 Mar 2027** attracts
₹1,000 / ₹5,000. On or before **31 Dec 2026**, nothing.

**Extension status.** As at **28 July 2026** the 31 July date had **not** been
extended; CBDT sources said there was no such discussion. Re-check before
relying on it — and prefer `ItrFilingDueDate` in the prefill or draft JSON,
which is the portal's own authoritative value.

**e-verification: 30 days** from transmission. Miss it and the return is treated
as never filed.

## EPF withdrawal

Exempt if there are **five years of continuous service** — service length, not
time to withdrawal. Transferred service via UAN counts.

If under five years, the accepted split is:

| Component | Head |
|---|---|
| Employer contribution + interest on it | Salary, s.17(3)(ii) |
| Interest on the employee's own contribution | Other Sources |
| Employee's own contribution | Not income, but 80C claimed on it is clawed back |

Reporting the gross amount in one head is common practice where the split isn't
available, and reconciles cleanly to 26AS. Note the s.111 / Rule 9 alternative
(see `portal-traps.md`) — it exists, it is the strict statutory machinery, and it
is usually far more expensive.

TDS u/s 192A is 10%, reported in Form 26Q, and lands in Schedule TDS-2.
